Executive GitHub and Open-Source Audit: Reusing versus Building from Scratch

Developing a deep learning pipeline, an illumination-invariant image preprocessor, a probability calibration layer, and a mobile-responsive community health worker (CHW) referral application from scratch within a 24-hour hackathon window presents a severe scheduling risk. A comprehensive audit of open-source repositories across GitHub and Hugging Face demonstrates that multiple teams have already constructed substantial components of this exact clinical pipeline1. However, no single repository satisfies every requirement of the problem statement out of the box, particularly the simultaneous execution of continuous hemoglobin () regression in grams per deciliter (), explicit skin-tone and lighting normalization, calibrated agreement diagnostics, and a structured clinical referral workflow3.

The optimal execution strategy for a 24-hour sprint is a "Fork-and-Graft" architecture. By cloning the most mature end-to-end production repository as the primary full-stack scaffold—which immediately delivers approximately 75% of the required application—the engineering team can graft specialized preprocessing, regression, and calibration modules from four complementary open-source projects1.

Deep Dive into the Primary Base Repository: RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection

The repository RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection, documented in the associated study AnemiaVision: Non-Invasive Anemia Detection via Smartphone Imagery Using EfficientNet-B3 with TrivialAugmentWide, Mixup, and Persistent Patient History Management, represents an ideal foundation for rapid prototyping4. Unlike standard academic repositories that only provide unorganized Jupyter notebooks, this codebase is structured as a modular web application designed specifically for rural community health workers screening 30 to 40 patients per hour without laboratory consumables4.

The codebase is cleanly decoupled into production-ready Python and JavaScript modules4. At the application layer, app.py and config.py power a Flask backend serving a mobile-first Jinja2 and Vanilla JavaScript interface featuring a dark-mode medical glassmorphism theme, rear-camera capture preference, and Chart.js analytics dashboards4. The backend also exposes a rate-limited REST endpoint (POST /api/predict) that accepts Base64-encoded images alongside patient metadata including name, age, gender, phone number, anatomical image type, and clinical notes4. Before any image reaches the neural network, image_validator.py acts as a pre-inference quality gatekeeper that verifies image dimensions, file format, and local quality hints4.

Within the deep learning core, model.py and train.py implement an EfficientNet-B3 backbone operating on  inputs4. Rather than relying on a standard single linear layer, the architecture employs a custom three-layer classification head following Global Average Pooling (): a Linear(1536, 512) layer with Batch Normalization, GELU activation, and Dropout(0.45), followed by a Linear(512, 256) layer with Batch Normalization, GELU activation, and Dropout(0.35), terminating in a Linear(256, 2) Softmax layer10. Crucially, train.py resolves a notorious failure mode in conjunctival convolutional neural network (CNN) training where validation loss oscillations at epoch 3 trigger premature early stopping at 44.9% validation accuracy7. By implementing accuracy-first early stopping alongside TrivialAugmentWide, RandomErasing, Mixup, and inverse-frequency class weighting, validation accuracy increases by 33.5 percentage points as the model converges over 80 epochs to reach 96.2%7. During inference, predict.py and gradcam.py execute calibrated probability pipelines and generate Grad-CAM spatial attention heatmaps rendered via an interactive comparison slider on the frontend, verifying that the network attends to the vascular bed rather than eyelashes or background artifacts4. Finally, storage_utils.py and pdf_report.py persist scan histories (such as public scan IDs formatted as AV-000042) into SQLite for local execution or PostgreSQL for cloud deployment, while generating downloadable clinical PDF reports via ReportLab4.

Critical Architectural Gaps Requiring Modification

Cloning RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection without modification leaves three notable vulnerabilities when evaluated against a strict clinical rubric. First, the repository lacks continuous hemoglobin () estimation and regression error reporting. Although the accompanying research paper mentions returning a hemoglobin range estimate10, the implemented neural network head (Linear(256, 2) -> Softmax) and the POST /api/predict JSON payload (prediction, confidence, risk_level) perform binary classification (Anemic versus Non-Anemic) rather than continuous  regression paired with Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and Bland-Altman agreement limits4.

Second, the baseline codebase lacks explicit skin-tone and color-space normalization. Relying purely on TrivialAugmentWide during training does not explicitly strip clinic-room lighting color casts or quantify model robustness across Fitzpatrick skin types7. Notably, in a subsequent June 2026 study (NeoJaundice-AI), the same author (Patel) introduced an explicit Grey-World White Balance and Individual Typology Angle (ITA) skin-tone normalization pipeline paired with a dual-head classification and regression architecture ()12. Grafting that exact mathematical upgrade back into RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection directly addresses the prompt's robustness and estimation requirements. Third, public conjunctiva datasets frequently suffer from data leakage due to augmented duplicates spanning across splits; integrating the duplicate-leakage audit protocol from Pai-88/pallor-hb and the patient-level split verification from JetX-GT/nail-anemia-detector is essential to establish scientific credibility1.

Clinical, Optical, and Algorithmic Mechanics of Non-Invasive Screening

The Physiological Basis of Non-Invasive Pallor Screening

Anemia is clinically characterized by a reduction in functional hemoglobin concentration (, measured in ) or circulating red blood cells, which impairs systemic oxygen transport to peripheral tissues14. While venous blood analysis via a Complete Blood Count (CBC) hematology analyzer remains the diagnostic gold standard, the procedure requires phlebotomy consumables, laboratory infrastructure, and turnaround time that frequently break down in rural or low-income primary care settings16.

In physical diagnosis, clinicians evaluate mucosal pallor across anatomical regions where microcapillary beds lie immediately beneath a translucent epithelium, specifically the palpebral conjunctiva (the inner lining of the lower eyelid), the fingernail bed, the palmar creases, and the tongue3. The palpebral conjunctiva and fingernail bed are uniquely suited for optical computer vision because their overlying tissue layers are virtually devoid of melanocytes11. Because hemoglobin strongly absorbs green and blue wavelengths () while reflecting red wavelengths (), the erythema (redness saturation relative to green and blue reflection) of these melanin-free vascular beds correlates directly with subcutaneous hemoglobin concentration5. Comparative clinical evaluations confirm that palpebral conjunctival pallor yields high diagnostic sensitivity (94.02%) and specificity (93.71%)21, while fingernail beds achieve superior CNN classification accuracy (97%) compared to palmar creases (83%)10.

Optical Confounders and Failure Modes in Uncontrolled Environments

Despite strong physiological foundations, translating smartphone photography into a quantitative hemoglobin meter encounters four severe optical and statistical confounders in unconstrained field settings. The first confounder is illumination drift and specular glare. Ambient light color temperature—ranging from warm tungsten indoor bulbs to cool outdoor daylight—shifts raw RGB channel intensities more drastically than a  drop in blood hemoglobin16. Furthermore, the tear film coating the wet palpebral conjunctiva and the smooth keratin plate of the fingernail produce intense specular reflections when struck by a camera flash or overhead spotlight, saturating all three RGB channels and mimicking severe pallor20.

The second confounder is camera sensor and Image Signal Processing (ISP) heterogeneity. Different smartphone models apply proprietary auto-exposure, non-linear gamma curves, and auto-white-balance matrices that alter absolute pixel intensities between devices6. The third confounder is the skin-tone contrast illusion. Even though the anterior conjunctiva and sub-ungual nail bed contain negligible melanin, unsegmented or loosely cropped images include surrounding periorbital skin or finger epidermis spanning Fitzpatrick Types I through VI12. When a smartphone camera captures a darkly pigmented patient (corresponding to a higher Massey score or lower Individual Typology Angle), the camera's auto-exposure meter increases sensor gain to brighten the dark surrounding skin, artificially over-exposing and washing out the conjunctiva or nail bed12. Moreover, deep CNNs trained on full-eye or full-finger crops without skin-tone normalization frequently learn spurious correlations between background skin pigmentation and cohort-level anemia prevalence rather than true mucosal erythema12.

The fourth confounder is the pure regression collapse trap. A revealing benchmark study (HemoScan, evaluated on 370 fingernail images using OpenCV segmentation and EfficientNet-B0) achieved 90.0% binary classification accuracy, yet its continuous hemoglobin regression suffered a Mean Absolute Error of  and an  score of , which is statistically indistinguishable from predicting the constant dataset mean6. When a deep network is trained exclusively with Mean Squared Error () on a few hundred noisy clinical images, illumination variance dominates the gradient updates, causing the regressor to minimize loss by predicting the global mean () across all samples6.

Core Technical Blueprint: Solving the Engineering Requirements

The end-to-end technical architecture operates across four sequential functional stages that sanitize raw smartphone photographs against environmental and physiological confounders before generating calibrated clinical outputs3. In Stage 1 (Smartphone Capture and Region-of-Interest Extraction), the application ingests palpebral conjunctiva or fingernail bed photographs, executes automated blur and format validation, and isolates the vascular region of interest while rejecting specular glare4. In Stage 2 (Optical and Skin-Tone Normalization), the image undergoes Grey-World white balance correction, HSV plus CLAHE illumination standardization, and CIE  Individual Typology Angle () scaling to neutralize clinic lighting casts and auto-exposure bias across diverse skin tones12. In Stage 3 (Hybrid AI Core), deep visual representations from the EfficientNet-B3 backbone are fused with 27 camera-invariant colorimetric ratios to drive dual task heads—a continuous hemoglobin regression head () and a binary anemia classification head—accompanied by a Grad-CAM spatial explainability overlay4. Finally, in Stage 4 (Community Health Worker Triage Application), raw predictions pass through Platt probability calibration and Bland-Altman agreement verification to generate a World Health Organization (WHO) risk-stratified referral PDF4.

Achieving Robustness to Lighting, Skin Tone, and Camera Differences

Within the RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection scaffold, inserting a dedicated normalization.py module immediately after image_validator.py establishes a deterministic four-step sequential pipeline3. As established in recent conjunctival and fingernail imaging studies, the exact sequence of preprocessing operations dictates feature integrity; applying color correction prior to background segmentation or omitting normalization alters the chromatic distribution seen by the classifier6.

First, the pipeline executes automated Region-of-Interest (ROI) cropping and specular glare masking. The mucosal bed of the palpebral conjunctiva or the nail plate is isolated using either pre-segmented masks during benchmark training, triangle thresholding and entropy grayscale extraction as deployed in CP-AnemiC, or an interactive touch-seed bounding box in the live mobile interface22. The extracted ROI is converted into the Hue-Saturation-Value (HSV) color space to identify specular reflection pixels caused by tear film or nail shine (where Brightness  and Saturation ), which are masked and reconstructed via OpenCV Telea inpainting so flash artifacts cannot depress mean erythema20.

Second, the pipeline performs Grey-World White Balance followed by HSV-CLAHE illumination normalization. Applying the Grey-World assumption rescales the Red, Green, and Blue channels so their spatial averages equalize to a neutral gray reference, stripping ambient clinic-room lighting color casts12. The white-balanced ROI is then transformed into the HSV color space where Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization (CLAHE) is applied strictly to the Value () illumination channel before converting back to RGB20. This procedure attenuates brightness differences across images without distorting the underlying chromaticity (Hue and Saturation) governed by hemoglobin concentration20.

Third, the pipeline implements dermatological skin-tone calibration via the Individual Typology Angle ()12. To neutralize camera auto-exposure bias and verify fairness across skin tones, the preprocessor samples the unsegmented periorbital or finger epidermis surrounding the ROI mask, converts those pixels into the CIE  color space via OpenCV (cv2.cvtColor), and computes the dermatological Individual Typology Angle12:

The resulting  classifies skin pigmentation into six dermatological tiers (Very Light through Dark, with values above  corresponding to lighter skin groups and negative angles corresponding to darker Fitzpatrick types IV–VI)12. Applying an ITA-based scaling factor to the ROI's  (lightness) and  (red-green opponent) channels compensates for sensor gain shifts induced by dark versus light surrounding skin, ensuring that the visual magnitude of pallor remains perceptually consistent across skin tones12.

Fourth, to achieve camera-sensor invariance, the preprocessor extracts 27 dimensionless colorimetric ratios and applies Correlation Alignment (CORAL)5. While distinct smartphone cameras vary in absolute channel sensitivity, dimensionless color ratios cancel out multiplicative sensor gain5. Following the feature engineering architecture of JetX-GT/nail-anemia-detector and the Eyes-Defy/CP-AnemiC cross-dataset studies, the module computes pallor brightness metrics, white-to-pink ratios, redness indices,  and  channel ratios, the Hemoglobin Redness Ratio (), spatial center-versus-edge vascular gradients, and grayscale entropy5. When transferring models across distinct geographic cohorts and cameras—such as training on Ghana's CP-AnemiC and evaluating on Italy and India's Eyes-Defy-Anemia—applying CORAL on the extracted feature space raises cross-dataset generalization by aligning second-order feature covariance matrices20.

Joint Hemoglobin Estimation and Anemia Classification Architecture

To prevent continuous hemoglobin regression from collapsing toward the dataset mean ( in naive single-head CNNs6), the model.py architecture in RAHULPATEL2002/anemia-detection must be upgraded to a Joint Classification and Regression Learning Framework12. This multi-task design mirrors the joint deep network established by the CP-AnemiC benchmark authors29, the multi-body-part BPANet architecture25, and the dual-head NeoJaundice-AI framework12.

In the feature fusion layer, the  deep embedding from the penultimate GELU layer of EfficientNet-B3 is concatenated with the  standardized vector of handcrafted colorimetric ratios and a 2-D anatomical body-part label encoding whether the input is a palpebral conjunctiva or fingernail bed image5. Incorporating explicit body-part labels alongside channel-spatial attention enables a single unified backbone to enhance the salient vascular features of each anatomical site25. The fused representation feeds two parallel task heads: a classification head consisting of a linear layer followed by Softmax that outputs calibrated class probabilities , and a continuous hemoglobin regression head consisting of a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with Softplus activation that outputs strictly positive hemoglobin concentration  in 11.

Coupling the regression objective to the classification boundary allows the continuous estimator to benefit from well-established classification gradients, ensuring stable handling of minority severe-anemia samples and driving continuous hemoglobin Mean Absolute Error down toward the benchmark state-of-the-art of 12.

Calibrated Accuracy, Agreement Diagnostics, and Error Reporting

Clinical evaluation of a non-invasive diagnostic aid requires distinguishing between raw neural network confidence and calibrated probability, as well as between linear correlation and clinical measurement interchangeability4.

Probability Calibration and Dual-Threshold Screening Modes

Deep neural networks trained with aggressive data augmentation such as Mixup and TrivialAugmentWide frequently produce under- or over-confident raw softmax probabilities7. Within predict.py, the classification logits should be passed through a post-hoc Platt Scaling or Temperature Scaling layer fitted on the validation split, accompanied by a Reliability Diagram, Expected Calibration Error (ECE), and Brier Score4.

Once probabilities are calibrated, the application should provide a Clinical Operating Mode Toggle tailored to the asymmetry of field screening errors5. In Standard Balanced Mode (operating at a probability threshold of ), the EfficientNet-B3 classifier replicates the AnemiaVision test benchmark, achieving 96.2% overall accuracy, 0.98 AUC-ROC, 96.0% sensitivity (True Positive rate), a 4.0% False Negative rate, and 95.0% specificity (True Negative rate)7. However, in rural community triage, missing an anemic child or pregnant woman (a False Negative) carries serious clinical risk, whereas a False Positive simply routes the patient for a confirmatory laboratory blood test5. By offering a High-Sensitivity Triage Mode that lowers the decision threshold to —as validated in JetX-GT/nail-anemia-detector—the system achieves 100% recall (zero missed anemic patients) at the expense of lower specificity (17.1%), providing an explicit safety lever for frontline screening5.

Continuous Hemoglobin Agreement via Bland-Altman Analysis

When evaluating continuous hemoglobin predictions ( in ) against laboratory reference measurements (), relying solely on the Pearson correlation coefficient () or  is clinically insufficient because two measurement methods can correlate strongly while exhibiting a persistent systematic offset36. Alongside Mean Absolute Error (MAE) and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)12, the evaluation suite (evaluate.py) must generate a Bland-Altman Agreement Plot (also termed Tukey's Mean-Difference Plot, implemented in Python via statsmodels.graphics.agreement.mean_diff_plot)42.

The Bland-Altman diagnostic plots the pairwise measurement average  along the horizontal axis against the pairwise difference  along the vertical axis36. The visualization incorporates three horizontal clinical reference lines: a zero-difference line () denoting zero error, the mean difference line () quantifying systematic bias, and the 95% Limits of Agreement () within which approximately 95% of differences between the smartphone model and the hematology analyzer reside36. Furthermore, regressing the pairwise differences  against the means  verifies the absence of proportional bias, confirming that estimation errors do not widen at severe anemia concentrations36.
